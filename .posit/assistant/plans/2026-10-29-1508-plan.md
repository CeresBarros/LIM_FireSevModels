## Caveats and assumptions (carry into the manuscript methods/discussion and AGENTS.md)
- **C1. Previous runs mixed the two sets.** The neighbourhood scope regex matched both the `ngbPropBurns_*m` and the ring-averaged `*_<ring>m` columns, so the earlier result that "buffer variables are no longer selected" probably reflects competition with correlated ring variables, not a lack of signal.
- **C2. GAIC with `k = log(n)` assumes residual independence given the model.** Spatial structure absorbed by the predictors (including the neighbourhood terms) is not a problem. Residual spatial autocorrelation at 30 m within fires lowers the effective sample size, so log(n) penalises too little and stepwise selection admits too many terms. The residual check in step 4 tests this assumption. If autocorrelation remains, report it, and rely on grouped CV for the comparison.
- **C3. The fire random effect does not handle within-fire autocorrelation.** `re(~1|FIRE_NAME)` captures between-fire differences only. For an unseen fire (CV test fires, holdout), predictions fall back to the population level, so grouped-CV and holdout metrics measure transfer to new fires without a fire-specific intercept.
- **C4. Select-once selection bias.** GAMLSS variables are selected on all calibration fires, so grouped-CV metrics of the fixed formulas are somewhat optimistic. Both sets get the same treatment, so the comparison stays fair, but absolute CV metrics should be reported as optimistic. Ring-ngb has many more candidate variables (≈120+ vs 7 for Old-ngb, decision 9), so it has more chances of spurious selection; the `log(n)` penalty and the CV check limit this but don't remove it.
- **C5. Stepwise selection depends on the search path.** The selected formulas can change with small changes to data or term order. Treat them as one good model, not as the unique best one.
- **C6. Different validation designs across model types.** GAMLSS uses select-once plus grouped CV; XGBoost uses nested grouped CV. XGBoost CV metrics are therefore less optimistic than GAMLSS ones, and the methods should say so when the two model types are compared.
- **C7. Few fires.** The holdout (1–2 fires) only catches large failures, and the number of grouped folds is limited. Report the spread across folds, not just means.
- **C8. The final model is not cross-validated itself.** Its credibility rests on the grouped-CV scores, the residual diagnostics, and the one-time holdout evaluation.
- **C9 (pending decision 10). Old-ngb is derived from the response.** `ngbPropBurns_*` is the proportion of neighbours with severity > 0 (`Neighbourhood_functions.R`, `.calculateNgbBurns`), i.e. from the burned/unburned status of the same fire's pixels. Ring-ngb uses only predictors. Old-ngb is a valid predictor only if burned/unburned status is known at prediction time (e.g. from simulated fire spread in LIM_PBH/LandR). Otherwise its CV and holdout scores are optimistic, and the Old-ngb vs Ring-ngb comparison favours Old-ngb. Also check whether the focal pixel is counted in its own buffer.
